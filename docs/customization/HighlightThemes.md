# Default Highlight Theme

The default read-along highlight theme, `defaultReadAlongHighlightThemes`, is built from the inks of fluorescent highlighters, and every color in it follows the rules below. See [Highlight Themes](./ReadAlong.md#highlight-themes) for how highlight themes are declared and applied.

## Combinations

The sentence and the word each have a style, set by a highlight preset or in the highlight subpanel, and the word is drawn on top of the sentence. A highlight theme declares colors by kind of style, from `DecorationStyleType`:

| Kind | Styles |
|---|---|
| fill | `Highlight`, `HighlightUnderline` |
| line | `Underline`, `Outline`, `Strikethrough` |
| text | `TextColor` |
| mask | `Mask`, for the sentence only: it dims everything but the sentence |

A fill renders its text in black or white, whichever contrasts most with it. The sentence has one color per kind, drawn on the page. The word has one color per kind for each kind of sentence style under it, which makes a matrix of combinations:

| Word ↓ · Sentence → | none or mask: `onPage` | fill: `onFill` | line: `onLine` | text: `onText` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fill | on the page | on the sentence fill | under the sentence’s line, which must stay visible on it | among the sentence’s colored text |
| line | on the page | on the sentence fill | on the page, next to the sentence’s line | on the page, around the sentence’s colored text |
| text | on the page, next to the page’s text | on the sentence fill, next to its black or white text | on the page, above the sentence’s line | on the page, next to the sentence’s colored text |

Each reading theme has 16 colors: the sentence’s fill, line, text and mask, and the 12 cells of the matrix. Each cell has to be readable on one surface and stand out from different neighbors, so a color that works in one cell fails in another: a text color that reads on the page may not read on a sentence fill, or may match the fill’s own text. The sentence’s colors constrain every cell drawn on them, so they are picked together with the word’s, see [How Colors Are Picked](#how-colors-are-picked).

## Updating the Colors

The colors are generated by `scripts/highlight-themes.mjs`, which applies the rules below to the reading themes in `src/preferences/models/theme.ts`. Don’t edit them by hand:

- `pnpm highlight-themes` prints the colors, and fails if the code or this page are out of date;
- `pnpm highlight-themes --write` writes them to `defaultReadAlongHighlightTheme` and to the tables in [Resulting Colors](#resulting-colors).

A new reading theme needs an entry in the script’s `THEME_CONFIG`, with the inks it can’t use and the sentence fills to try. Inks, thresholds and dim factors are constants at the top of the script.

## How Inks Are Used

- Word fills are inks, as they are.
- Lines and text colors are inks, darkened toward black on light surfaces, or lightened toward white on dark ones, by steps of 1% until they reach 3:1 for lines and 4.5:1 for text colors against what they are drawn on: the page, or the sentence fill for `onFill`. An ink that already reaches it is used as it is, e.g. on dark pages.
- Yellow and orange are never darkened, as they turn olive and brown: on light pages, they are only used as fills.
- The sentence fill is yellow on light pages, a slightly more saturated `#ffef3a` on sepia, as its page is close to yellow. On dark pages, it is a dim ink, the ink with its channels scaled down to 28%, 34% or 40%, that renders its text in white, so that inks can be used at full strength on it. `contrast3` inverts the sentence instead, filling it with the theme’s black text color.
- A contrast theme never uses the ink of its own color: no yellow on `contrast1`, whose text is yellow, no blue on `contrast2`, whose page is navy, and no green on `contrast3`, whose page is green.

## What Each Color Must Satisfy

Two colors are distinct when they reach 3:1, or when their hues are at least 90° apart, or at least 45° apart with 1.5:1. A saturated color is distinct from black, white or gray text at 2:1. Red and green are never distinct by hue alone, for readers with color vision deficiencies.

| Color | Must |
|---|---|
| `utterance.fill` | render its text, in black or white, at 4.5:1 |
| `utterance.line` | reach 3:1 on the page, and be distinct from the page’s text |
| `utterance.text` | reach 4.5:1 on the page, and be distinct from the page’s text |
| `word.fill.onPage` | be distinct from the page |
| `word.fill.onFill` | be distinct from the sentence fill |
| `word.fill.onLine` | keep the sentence’s line at 3:1 on it, not be red against a green line or the reverse, and be distinct from the page |
| `word.fill.onText` | be distinct from the sentence’s text color |
| `word.line.onPage` | reach 3:1 on the page |
| `word.line.onFill` | reach 3:1 on the sentence fill |
| `word.line.onLine` | reach 3:1 on the page, and be distinct from the sentence’s line |
| `word.line.onText` | reach 3:1 on the page, and be distinct from the sentence’s text color |
| `word.text.onPage` | reach 4.5:1 on the page, and be distinct from the page’s text |
| `word.text.onFill` | reach 4.5:1 on the sentence fill, and be distinct from the fill’s black or white text |
| `word.text.onLine` | reach 4.5:1 on the page, and be distinct from the page’s text and the sentence’s line |
| `word.text.onText` | reach 4.5:1 on the page, and be distinct from the sentence’s text color |

## How Colors Are Picked

For each reading theme, every combination of sentence fill, sentence line ink and sentence text ink is tried. For each combination, the word’s fill, line and text color each get a main ink, and an alternate ink used only in the cells where the main one does worse. Each cell is scored by how close it gets to its requirements, and the combination whose worst cell scores highest is kept, then the one with the highest total, then the one using the fewest inks.
